Cellsea

an eyeless thing in an endless sea

You are a handful of hexagonal cells drifting in an expanse with no edges. Build a body at the bench, then go take cells off of everything else that lives out here.

Hearts keep you alive — lose them all and you dissolve. Normal cells are your muscle; strip them and you crawl. Armour is tough but heavy. Spikes are spines you bolt onto any cell — the cell underneath keeps its job — and they shear off whatever they touch, though armour can snap them. Flagella push away from themselves — mount them at the back to go forward, and keep open water behind them, because a buried tail pushes nothing.

A jet trades the tail for gas: weaker when buried, but it always vents through a seam, and there is nothing hanging off it to cut. A poison gland spits an orb out of any clear side — whatever it lands on is gone, and the rot spreads from there for a couple of rings before it burns out.

Three more are worth the biomass. An angry neuron is dead weight until a detach seam cuts its piece loose — then that piece hunts on its own and will never turn on you. An exploding cell bursts when anything living touches it. Put all three in one pod and you have built a suicide drone.
